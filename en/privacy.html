Privacy policy

Version 2 · Effective 5 September 2026 · Applies to Emimate for Android, package com.emimate.app, version 1.5.0 and later

Emimate collects no personal data. All information recorded by the user remains in the app's private storage, on the device. The developer neither receives nor holds any data, and has no technical means of obtaining it.

One copy does leave the device, and it is the operating system that takes it, not the app: with the backup switched on, Android keeps the diary in the user's own Google Drive, so that losing or replacing a phone does not mean losing the diary. The copy stays in the user's account, which only they can reach; the developer does not receive it and has no knowledge of it. The feature is on by default and is switched off in the app's settings. Section 5 sets it out in full.

1. Controller and scope

Emimate is published by an independent developer, reachable at gprovenz.dev@gmail.com. Because the app transmits no information and the developer operates no server, no processing of personal data is carried out by the developer, and there is accordingly no processing for which a lawful basis under Regulation (EU) 2016/679 would need to be identified. The diary data is processed solely by the user, on the user's own device and under the user's own control.

This policy concerns the Emimate app for Android. The emimate.it website is the subject of a separate document, the cookie policy, which describes how it operates and records the absence of cookies and tracking tools.

3. The technical basis for the statements above

Emimate does not request the INTERNET permission. Android enforces this: without that permission the operating system does not allow the app to open any network connection. This is not a setting that can be altered silently on an installed app; it is a declaration contained in the manifest, which anyone can extract from the installed package and verify.

Android's automatic backup is on (allowBackup="true"). It is a feature of the operating system and not of the app, which, having no INTERNET permission, could not transmit the file itself. What is copied is declared in the manifest, in backup_rules.xml and data_extraction_rules.xml, and is equally open to inspection: only the two CSV files the app writes for the purpose, and not the database.

The app integrates no advertising network, no analytics library, no crash reporting service and no third-party SDK. No party is informed that the app has been opened, or when.

5. Transfers arranged by the user

The diary leaves the device in two circumstances only, both of them the user's to decide.

a) The backup copy in the user's own Google Drive

Where the feature is on, Android periodically takes two CSV files — the diary and the settings — and places them in the space reserved for the app inside the Google Drive attached to the user's account. The operation is carried out by the operating system, at a moment of its choosing, when the device is idle, charging and connected to Wi-Fi. The app takes no part in it and, having no INTERNET permission, would not be able to carry it out.

The copy is protected by the credentials of the user's Google account and, on devices running Android 9 and later, is end-to-end encrypted with the device's screen lock, so that not even Google can read its contents. The developer does not reach it, is not told of it, and has no technical means of obtaining it. Google's handling of the copy is governed by Google's own terms and privacy policy, which the user is party to by virtue of holding the account.

The feature is on by default, because losing a phone is the event that most often loses a diary. It is switched off under Settings → Backup, where the date of the last copy is also shown, along with how often one may be taken. Switched off, the app stops updating the two files and Android has nothing left to take.

The copy returns when the app is installed on a new device. At that point the app asks whether to restore the diary, giving the date of the copy and the number of attacks in it; if the answer is no, the copy is deleted, and so is neither put back into the diary nor kept on the device.

b) Exporting a file

Emimate allows the diary to be exported in CSV format and a report to be produced in PDF format for the user's doctor, and allows either to be passed to another app through the Android share function. This occurs solely at the user's request: the user determines the moment, the period covered, where the file is placed and who receives it.

Once exported, the file is an ordinary document under the user's control and is no longer governed by this policy. Its subsequent handling is subject to the terms of the app, mail service or storage service selected.

6. Retention period

The diary remains on the device until deleted by the user. There is no expiry and no automatic clean-up, and there is no data for the developer to retain or erase.

Where the backup is on, the copy placed in the user's Google Drive remains for as long as Google's terms provide, and is governed by them. Google removes the copy after a period during which the app has not been used, or once the app is uninstalled from the device.

7. Erasure of data

  • A single attack: open it in the app and select Delete.
  • The whole diary: from the app's settings, under Delete → Delete all data. The action cannot be undone and says so. A box, unticked by default, extends the deletion to the backup copy; ticking it asks a second time, since that half of the action admits of no remedy.
  • Uninstalling: Android removes the app's private storage at the same time. The same result is obtained, with the app kept installed, from the Android settings under Apps → Emimate → Storage → Clear storage.
  • The copy placed in Google Drive: no app has the power to remove it directly, and Emimate is no exception. Deleting the backup from the settings empties the app's own files, and Android replaces the copy in Drive with them at the next backup. The copy is likewise removed by Google once the app is uninstalled, or through the settings of the user's Google account.

Restoring a backup never entails an erasure: it adds the attacks contained in the file, updates those the file describes differently, and leaves the remainder unaltered. Erasure is always an explicit act.

8. Rights of the data subject

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 affords the data subject the right of access to their personal data, together with the rights to rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, objection and portability. As the developer holds none of the user's data, those rights are exercised directly on the device and require no request to be submitted to anyone: the diary is fully viewable in the app, editable entry by entry, erasable as set out above, and exportable at any time to a CSV file in a documented, open format that any spreadsheet can read.

The data subject also has the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. In Italy that authority is the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (garanteprivacy.it).

9. Transfers to third countries

The developer makes no transfer, either within or outside the European Economic Area, as the developer receives no data.

Where the backup is on, the placing of the copy in the user's Google Drive is carried out by Google, which may hold it on its own servers, including servers outside the European Economic Area. That transfer is between the user and Google, under the relationship already existing between them by virtue of the account, and is governed by Google's privacy policy. On devices running Android 9 and later the copy is end-to-end encrypted with the device's screen lock, so its contents are not readable by Google. Anyone wishing to avoid that transfer altogether switches the backup off in the app's settings.

10. Children

Emimate is not directed at children and collects no information from any user, of any age.
